Section 2

The 6 Factors That Determine Your Optimal Claiming Age

1

Your Income Gap

How much monthly income do your other sources (savings, pensions, annuities) already cover? If you have a large gap, delaying may not be realistic.

2

Your Health & Family Longevity

The break-even math changes dramatically based on life expectancy. A family history of longevity favors delay. Chronic health conditions may not.

3

Your Spouse's Benefit & Age

Spousal and survivor benefits create scenarios where one spouse should claim early and the other late. Most couples never model this.

4

Your Tax Situation

Claiming Social Security while still working or withdrawing from pre-tax accounts can push up to 85% of your benefit into taxable income. Timing matters.

5

Your Other Income Sources

If you have guaranteed income from a pension or annuity covering your floor, you have more flexibility to delay. If not, you may need Social Security sooner to avoid depleting savings.

6

IRMAA Exposure

Higher income from poor Social Security timing can trigger Medicare premium surcharges most people don't see coming.

Section 4

The 3 Most Expensive Social Security Mistakes

⚠

Claiming at 62 "just to get something"

Without modeling the lifetime difference. For someone with a $2,500/mo FRA benefit, claiming at 62 reduces it to $1,750/mo — permanently. Over a 25-year retirement, that's a staggering difference in total income received.

Potential Cost: $225,000+ in lost lifetime income
⚠

Both spouses claiming at the same time

Without optimizing survivor benefits. When one spouse dies, the surviving spouse keeps the higher of the two benefits. If neither delayed, the survivor benefit may not be enough to cover expenses alone.

Potential Cost: $50,000–$150,000+ in reduced survivor income
⚠

Ignoring the tax torpedo

Social Security + pre-tax withdrawals combined can push 85% of your benefit into taxable income AND trigger IRMAA surcharges. The marginal tax rate in this "torpedo zone" can exceed 40%.

Potential Cost: $40,000–$100,000+ in unnecessary taxes over retirement
